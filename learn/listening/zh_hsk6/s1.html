第 1 段　Many think coffee keeps you awake. In fact its effect on sleep differs from person to person: some drink a cup in the evening and still sleep well; others have one in the afternoon and can't sleep at night. Experts suggest people sensitive to coffee stop drinking it after noon.

Many think coffee keeps you awake. In fact its effect on sleep differs from person to person: some drink a cup in the evening and still sleep well; others have one in the afternoon and can't sleep at night. Experts suggest people sensitive to coffee stop drinking it after noon.

Research finds that working in total silence isn't necessarily most efficient. Moderate background sound — like people talking quietly in a café — can actually make people more creative. But if it's too loud, you can't concentrate.

第 9 段　In recent years many cities have seen a new kind of public space: the "city study". Usually set up in neighbourhoods, parks or shopping centres, they're small but quiet and brightly lit, with thousands of books, and residents can go in to read and study for free. Compared with traditional libraries they have several clear features: first, they're closer to home — a walk of ten-odd minutes; second, many are open 24 hours, so you can go after work; and borrowing and returning are done yourself at machines. City studies have made reading an everyday thing: some prepare for exams there, some quietly read a novel, and parents bring their children to read together. Experts think they not only serve residents but help more people get into the habit of reading.
逐字稿（聽完再看）
旁白近年来，很多城市出现了一种新的公共空间——“城市书房”。它们一般开在社区、公园或者商场里，面积不大，却环境安静、灯光明亮，放着几千本图书，市民可以免费进去看书、学习。 和传统的图书馆相比，城市书房有几个明显的特点。首先，它们离居民的家更近，走路十几分钟就能到。其次，很多城市书房二十四小时开放，晚上下班以后也能去。另外，借书、还书都可以自己用机器完成，非常方便。 城市书房的出现，让读书变成了一件很平常的事。有人在这里准备考试，有人在这里安静地读一本小说，还有家长带着孩子来这里一起看书。有专家认为，城市书房不仅方便了市民，也有助于让更多的人养成爱读书的习惯。

第 10 段　Have you ever been exhausted yet unable to sleep, your mind going over the day's events? This has a lot to do with your habits before bed. Experts point out that many people look at their phones before sleep, and the light from the screen affects the brain, making it harder to fall asleep. Eating too much, drinking strong tea or doing hard exercise before bed also hurts sleep quality. So how can you sleep well? Experts suggest putting the phone down an hour before bed, taking a hot bath or listening to relaxing music so body and brain slowly unwind. One more important thing: go to bed and get up at the same time every day, weekends included. Over time your body forms a fixed rhythm, and you'll naturally feel sleepy at the right time.

第 11 段　In some big Chinese cities a new job has appeared: the "hospital companion". Their work is to accompany people who find it hard to go to hospital alone — elderly people, young people working away from home and so on. A hospital visit usually involves many steps — registering, queuing, tests, collecting medicine — and for some old people even finding the right place in the hospital is hard. Companions book appointments in advance, stay beside the patient during the visit, note down what the doctor says, and afterwards explain the points to note to the patient and family. The job is welcomed by many families, especially old people whose children are far away. But it faces problems: there's no unified service standard yet, and fees vary widely. Experts suggest the authorities issue regulations soon so the industry develops healthily.

第 12 段　Playing on the beach, you may have picked up round pieces of glass with very smooth surfaces. They're called "sea glass". You may not expect it, but these pretty "gems" come from rubbish people threw into the sea. Years ago people often threw glass bottles and cups straight into the sea. Tossed about by the waves and rubbing against sand and stones for decades or even a century, the sharp edges slowly became rounded and the surface looked as if polished. Today sea glass is something many people like to collect; some make necklaces, earrings and other small ornaments from it. But as environmental awareness rises, fewer people throw glass into the sea, and since many bottles are now plastic, sea glass is becoming ever rarer.
逐字稿（聽完再看）
旁白在海边玩儿的时候，你可能捡到过一些圆圆的、表面很平的玻璃。它们被称为“海玻璃”。你也许想不到，这些漂亮的“小石头”，其实来自人们扔进大海的垃圾。 很多年前，人们常常把玻璃瓶、玻璃杯直接扔进大海。这些玻璃在海水里被不停地冲来冲去，和沙子、石头碰来碰去，经过几十年甚至上百年，原本很容易伤人的边角慢慢变得圆圆的，表面也变得很平。 如今，海玻璃成了很多人喜欢收集的东西，有人还用它们做成项链、耳环等漂亮的小东西。不过，随着人们环保意识的提高，往海里扔玻璃的人越来越少，再加上现在很多瓶子都是塑料做的，海玻璃也变得越来越少见了。

第 13 段　When chatting online, many people like to use cute or funny pictures to show their mood: "stickers". A smiling face or a winking kitten often expresses what you mean more precisely than a long paragraph. Why are they so popular? Research suggests that face to face we read others' emotions from their expressions and voices, but online, text alone easily leads to misunderstanding. Stickers make up for this, making online exchanges more relaxed and more human. But people of different ages sometimes understand the same sticker differently: a "smile", for example, seems friendly to older people, while some young people take it to mean "I don't want to chat any more". So be careful with stickers when chatting with elders or people you don't know well.

旁白在网上聊天时，很多人喜欢用一些可爱或者有趣的图片来表达自己的心情，这些图片被称为“表情包”。一个笑脸、一只笑着的小猫，往往比一大段文字更能准确地表达说话人的意思。 表情包为什么这么受欢迎？有研究认为，面对面说话的时候，人们可以通过对方的表情、声音来理解对方的情绪；而在网上，只看文字很容易产生误会。表情包正好弥补了这个不足，让网上的交流变得更轻松、更温暖。 不过，不同年龄的人对同一个表情包的理解，有时并不一样。比如，一个“微笑”的表情，年纪大的人觉得是友好，有些年轻人却认为它有“不想再聊了”的意思。所以，和长辈或者不太熟悉的人聊天时，使用表情包最好小心一些。

女李师傅，您做面点已经三十多年了，当初为什么会选择这个工作？
Master Li, you've made dough foods for over thirty years. Why did you choose this trade?
男我家里三代人都是做面点的。小时候我常常在厨房里看父亲做面、包包子，觉得特别有意思。十六岁那年，我就正式跟着父亲学技术了。

女听说您做的包子特别有名，有什么特别的方法吗？
I hear your buns are famous. What's the secret?
男其实没什么特别的方法，就是用心。面要做够时间，里面的肉和菜要用当天买的新鲜的，每一个包子都要包得一样大、一样漂亮。很多年轻人觉得这些细节不重要，可是味道往往就差在这些地方。
There's no real secret — just care. Knead the dough long enough, use fresh meat and vegetables bought that day for the filling, and give every bun eighteen pleats. Many young people think such details don't matter, but that's exactly where the taste is lost.
女现在很多人更喜欢吃西式点心，您担心传统面点会没人喜欢吗？
Many people now prefer Western pastries. Do you worry traditional dough foods will be neglected?
男有一点儿担心，但我觉得关键还是要创新。这两年，我们推出了一些新口味，还做了小动物形状的包子，很受孩子们欢迎。传统不是不能改变的，要让年轻人喜欢，才能真正传下去。
A little, but I think the key is innovation. In the last two years we've launched some new flavours and made buns shaped like little animals, which children love. Tradition isn't fixed; only if young people like it can it really be passed on.

女是因为我母亲。她一个人住在老家，每次打视频电话，都要我在电话里一步一步地教她怎么操作，常常教了半天她还是不会。我就想，能不能做一个更简单的软件，让老人一看就懂。
Because of my mother. She lives alone back home, and every video call I had to teach her step by step how to work it, often for ages without success. So I wondered whether I could make a simpler app that old people would understand at a glance.

How is it different from ordinary apps?
女最大的不同是简单。字很大，要点的地方很少，最常用的功能，比如打电话、看天气，打开就能看到。另外，孩子还可以在自己的手机上远程帮父母设置，不用再在电话里一句一句地教了。

女最难的是了解老人真正的需要。一开始，我们以为功能越多越好，后来去社区找了一百多位老人试用，才发现他们最需要的其实只有几个功能。所以我们删掉了一大半，没想到反而更受欢迎了。
